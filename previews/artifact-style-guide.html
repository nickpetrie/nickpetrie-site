The essay’s argument, short enough to read in the feed, with the card doing the stopping.

Nick PetrieDigital Director at The i Paper

Bridge roles need to die. Not because the problems they solve don't matter. Coordination between editorial and product matters more than ever. But these roles have become the sticking plaster we reach for instead of doing the harder work: restructuring teams, aligning incentives, building shared goals. A lot of the people I coach are in bridge roles. They see the dysfunction more clearly than anyone, and hold things together through sheer force of will. Because they're so good at it, the business never has to fix the underlying problem. If you create a bridge role, plan how to retire it on the day you create it. What's the longest-running "temporary" role in your organisation? nickpetrie.co/bridge-roles-need-to-die

  1. The first line is the claim.LinkedIn shows about two lines before “see more”. Make them the argument, not a warm-up.
  2. Two to four short paragraphs.Why it matters, one concrete example from the work, what to do. 80 to 200 words.
  3. End on a question.One people can answer from their own experience. The link goes last, on its own line.
  4. The card stops the scroll.Portrait (4:5) takes the most room in the feed. Same title as the essay, so people recognise it on the site.
  5. No hashtags needed.If you use them: up to three, specific, at the end.

House style

The small decisions, made once.

SpellingBritish: organisation, prioritise, programme, behaviour.
CaseSentence case for headlines, titles and buttons. Kickers are capitalised by the design, never typed in caps.
Dates and money23 January 2026 · 7pm, 7.30pm · £250, £1
NumbersOne to nine in words, 10 and up in figures. Always figures for data and money.
PunctuationPrefer a colon or full stop to a dash. Curly quotes in designs.
NamesThe i Paper · The Times and Sunday Times · Reuters · News Product Alliance, then NPA · When News Meets Product, then WNMP where readers know it.
LinksLink text says where it goes. Never “click here”.
HashtagsNone, or up to three specific ones at the end.
AvoidHype (game-changer, unlock, supercharge) · “I’m humbled to announce” · one-line-per-paragraph cliffhangers · emoji in designs · naming and shaming people or employers.
