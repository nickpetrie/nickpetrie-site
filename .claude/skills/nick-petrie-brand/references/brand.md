# Nick Petrie and When News Meets Product: brand, voice and facts

Brand v0.3 (28 October 2026). The same system runs the website (nickpetrie.co), the newsletter (beehiiv) and social posts. Source tokens: `brand/v0.3/brand-tokens.json` in the `nickpetrie/nickpetrie-site` repo.

## Two marks, one system

- **Nick Petrie** is the person: the NP mark plus "Nick Petrie" in Source Sans 3 Semibold. Ink or paper only. Never a yellow NP.
- **When News Meets Product (WNMP)** is the newsletter: the masthead (paired rules, "When News Meets Product" in Source Sans 3 Bold, "by Nick Petrie"). Yellow belongs to WNMP.
- On social, use the NP lockup for essays, quotes and mentoring, and the WNMP masthead (on yellow) for newsletter issues and subscribe prompts.

Rules: yellow is never text on paper (it fails contrast). Yellow text is fine on ink, for emphasis only. Links are the text colour with an underline. One accent per design: don't put yellow rules, a highlight and a yellow kicker all on one canvas.

## Type

- **Source Serif 4 Display** for headlines at 40px and above. Regular weight only; size does the work, not bold.
- **Source Serif 4** for body text and anything serif under 40px. Italic for titles of works and the occasional stress.
- **Source Sans 3** (Regular, Semibold, Bold) for the UI voice: labels, kickers, bylines, buttons, URLs.
- Kickers and labels: Source Sans 3 Bold, uppercase, letter-spaced (`.06–.07em`). Everything else is sentence case.
- Email falls back to Georgia (body) and Arial.

## The paired rules

A thick bar over a thin one (48 × 27: bars 11 and 5, gap 11). It is the WNMP motif and the house accent: above a headline, as section dividers, as the bullet on lists. Yellow on paper or ink; ink on yellow. Use it once per design.

## Voice

Nick writes as a practitioner talking to peers: someone who has done the job (The Times, Reuters, The i Paper) and is honest about how hard it is. The reader is trying to change a news organisation from the inside.

**He sounds like:**
- **Direct.** Leads with the argument. Headlines are claims, not topics: "Bridge roles need to die", "Strategy isn't sexy. It just means having a plan", "No thing can save journalism".
- **Plain.** Short sentences, everyday words, British English. Jargon only when the audience uses it, and explained when it isn't obvious.
- **First person, warm, a bit wry.** "Hello. I've been quiet for a while. Two kids and a full time job will do that." Self-deprecating, never sarcastic about people.
- **Concrete.** Real examples (The Times' editions, The Verge's redesign, Tesla's plan), numbers with context, metaphors from life (bouldering, town planners, buckets on a sinking ship).
- **Honest about difficulty.** "All of these are easy to write and hard to do." Names trade-offs.
- **Constructive.** Every critique ends with what to do: a numbered list of actions, or a question.
- **Open.** Ends by inviting replies: "What would you do differently?"

**He doesn't:**
- hype ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock", "supercharge"), or predict doom for clicks;
- write LinkedIn-bro ("I'm humbled to announce", one-line-per-paragraph cliffhangers, "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in");
- attack named people or employers, or share anything confidential from work;
- claim credit for team work: "we" for team achievements, "I" for opinions;
- use emoji in designs; in posts, at most one, and usually none.

**Tone by format:**

| Format | Tone |
|---|---|
| Essay or newsletter | Personal. Each issue is one thought, a couple of interesting links and an invitation to join the discussion. |
| LinkedIn post | The essay's argument in 80 to 200 words. First line is the claim. Two to four short paragraphs. One question or one link. |
| Quote card | One line he'd stand behind on its own. Verbatim from the essay. |
| Mentoring | Warm and practical. Pay what you can, stated plainly and meant. |
| Replies and comments | Generous, curious, brief. |

## House style

- British spelling: organisation, prioritise, programme, behaviour.
- Sentence case for headlines, titles and buttons. Kickers in caps via styling, never typed in caps.
- Dates: 23 January 2026. Times: 7pm, 7.30pm. Money: £250, £1.
- Numbers: one to nine in words, 10 and up in figures; always figures for data and money.
- Punctuation: prefer a colon or a full stop to a dash. Single spaces. Curly quotes in designs.
- Names: The i Paper, The Times and Sunday Times, Reuters, News Product Alliance (NPA after first use), When News Meets Product (WNMP after first use, and only where readers know it).
- No "click here". Link text says where it goes.
- Hashtags: none, or up to three specific ones at the end (#newsproduct #journalism #media).
